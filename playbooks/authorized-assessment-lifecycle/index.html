    Authorized assessment lifecycle¶

    This playbook is the common spine for more specialized playbooks. It organizes an assessment around explicit authorization, testable hypotheses, proportional validation, evidence, cleanup, and communication.

    Authorization

    Written authorization and the current rules of engagement control every phase. If the observed environment differs materially from the approved scope, stop the affected activity and resolve the discrepancy.

    1. Define the engagement¶

    Establish the business objective before choosing tools or tests.

    • Identify the decision the assessment must support.
    • Name in-scope systems, identities, facilities, applications, APIs, cloud tenants, and third parties.
    • Record explicit exclusions, prohibited actions, rate limits, and production constraints.
    • Define the assessment type: vulnerability assessment, penetration test, assumed breach, adversary simulation, or another clearly scoped exercise.
    • Agree on success criteria, evidence requirements, reporting format, and retest expectations.

    Exit criteria¶

    Do not proceed until authorization, scope, dates, operators, contacts, data handling, emergency stop, and cleanup ownership are documented.

    2. Prepare the operation¶

    Create the minimum infrastructure and records necessary for safe work.

    • Confirm operator identities and approved test sources.
    • Synchronize clocks and establish an activity-log format.
    • Test communication and emergency escalation paths.
    • Prepare approved encrypted evidence storage.
    • Identify fragile services and monitoring expectations.
    • Establish an artifact inventory and naming convention.
    • Validate that test accounts, lab replicas, backups, or rollback methods work where applicable.

    3. Model the environment¶

    Build an initial picture using provided documentation and low-impact observations. Treat unknowns as explicit hypotheses.

    Question Evidence sought Why it changes the plan
    What assets and trust boundaries exist? Inventories, diagrams, observed services Prevents accidental scope expansion
    Which identities and privilege tiers exist? Role definitions, approved account context Guides authorization testing safely
    Where is sensitive data processed? Data flows and classifications Controls evidence collection and impact validation
    Which controls should observe testing? Logging and detection coverage Supports purple-team and detection objectives

    Update the model when evidence contradicts it. Do not force observations into the initial plan.

    4. Prioritize hypotheses¶

    Turn the threat model and observations into testable statements. Prioritize by expected impact, exposure, plausibility, coverage value, and operational risk.

    For each hypothesis, define:

    1. the precondition being tested;
    2. the least disruptive validation method;
    3. the expected positive and negative observations;
    4. the evidence threshold;
    5. side effects and cleanup;
    6. the stop or approval condition.

    Use specialized Technique pages for procedures. A failed tool command is not a negative security result unless the technique and its assumptions were validated.

    5. Execute and adapt¶

    Work from low-impact discovery toward narrowly scoped validation. Maintain an activity log while testing, not after the fact.

    After each material observation, ask:

    • Does this remain in scope?
    • Does it change the risk or safety model?
    • Is the result an observation, an inference, or demonstrated impact?
    • What is the next smallest test that reduces uncertainty?
    • Is additional approval required?
    • What artifacts were created?

    Pause when scope is ambiguous, safety assumptions fail, sensitive data appears unexpectedly, a third party is involved, or service stability changes.

    6. Validate impact proportionally¶

    Demonstrate only the impact needed to support the objective. Prefer reversible proofs and synthetic data. Avoid collecting real data, changing business records, disrupting services, or expanding privileges when a smaller proof is sufficient.

    Record both demonstrated impact and credible but untested impact. Never present the latter as observed fact.

    7. Preserve evidence¶

    For every conclusion, retain the minimum evidence that establishes who, what, when, where, and how. Follow the note-taking and evidence standard.

    Evidence should be attributable, time-ordered, integrity-protected where required, and stored according to the engagement's classification and retention rules.

    8. Clean up and reconcile¶

    Compare the artifact inventory with the activity log. Remove approved testing artifacts, restore changed state, revoke temporary access, and document anything that cannot be removed.

    Cleanup is complete only after verification. An attempted deletion is not proof of restoration.

    9. Analyze and report¶

    Group symptoms by root cause and connect individual findings into attack paths where the evidence supports it. Each material claim should trace to protected evidence.

    Reports should distinguish:

    • observed condition;
    • demonstrated impact;
    • plausible additional impact;
    • affected scope and uncertainty;
    • root cause;
    • prioritized remediation;
    • retest criteria.

    10. Close and improve¶

    Confirm evidence transfer or destruction, outstanding cleanup, retest ownership, and final communications. Conduct a retrospective and promote reusable lessons into the handbook:

    • concepts into Knowledge;
    • focused procedures into Techniques;
    • workflow improvements into Playbooks;
    • communication patterns into Reporting;
    • diagnostic shortcuts into Reference.

    Completion checklist¶

    • Scope and authorization remained current
    • Material actions are represented in the activity log
    • Claims trace to sufficient evidence
    • Sensitive data collection was minimized
    • Artifacts and state changes were reconciled
    • Findings include root cause and retest criteria
    • Residual risk and uncertainty are explicit
    • Reusable learning was extracted into the handbook
